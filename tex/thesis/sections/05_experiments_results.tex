This chapter displays the final evidence of the experiment for our RAGPoison benchmark. The results should be read only as a local evaluation and not universal measurements for all LLM-powered recommender systems as we only cover a small dataset and a small pool of frontier public models fixed at top-$k=10$ with three attack families, two retrieval settings, two ranking settings, two victim model configurations, and five attacker model configurations \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo,wu2024llmrecsurvey,zhao2024llmrecsystems}.


\subsection{Experimental Setup, Dataset, and Corpus}

As explained in Section~\ref{sec:methodology} The experiment in its current completed state is derived from the dataset MovieLens 100k with the whole catalogue prepared,indexed in bulk then in vector on our vector database Elasticsearch and poisoned depending on the attack. the experiment is closed meaning the results achieved are relevant enough for the following analysis steps of this thesis. Each of the following presented 15 runs composing the full matrix run were executed on all 943 user. Most of the data that resulted in the experiment here is discussed through the split tables in this chapter \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo,harper2015movielens,elastic_docs_es}.

The corpus holds 15 runs because we use three attack families across five attackers, We made a choice to value the attacker more than the victim as the attackers Job is more relevant too our study than the victims one while being cost effective. The matrix tries to balance out all methods and the modularity that the RAGPoison benchmark allows so we get five entries for each attack family (targeted promotion, prompt injection, and untargeted degradation). In the attempt to get the most telling results that to this day are still relevant, targeted promotion and untargeted degradation ran with hybrid retrieval  while prompt injection used dense retrieval for the setup. We are thus allowed to see the extent of each attack in their most effective scenarios and utilizing the full extent of options RAGPoison offers \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo}.

Though we discuss the metrics meaning for each of the attack and the relevance of certain metrics for each family, in the spirit of comparing the effectiveness of the attacks equally we will run all of them under the same metric conditions. HR, MRR and NDCG will show us the recommendation quality on every run and ASR will describe if the targeted movie reached into the set top-10. The irrelevant metrics of a family will be briefly discussed and only used to show how different attacks can harm a users recommendation in their own way \cite{herlocker2004evaluating,wang2013ndcg,ragpoisonlab_repo}

Each row will hold the attack, retrieval mode and the means of each metric, when available. The deltas of each metric will follow this formula \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo}

\subsection{Attack-Family Results}


\subsubsection{Targeted Promotion Mini Case Study}

Targeted promotion is the clearest case of an exposure attack in the final matrix. The attacker does not need to destroy the whole recommendation list. The objective is more surgical: make the target movie appear in the top-$k$ output more often than it would under the clean index. This is why the mean $\Delta$ASR of $0.348463$ is the main result for this family, while the smaller negative quality deltas describe collateral movement around that target. The attack is therefore closer to manipulating visibility than to breaking all recommendation quality \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo,nguyen2024manipulatingrecsys,wang2024recpoisonsurvey}.

Run R09 is the strongest targeted-promotion row in the matrix, with $\Delta$ASR of $0.433723$. This row matters because it shows the target objective succeeding under the same MovieLens-derived user base and top-$k=10$ evaluation logic as the other rows. The quality metrics do not collapse in the same way as the untargeted-degradation family, but that does not make the attack harmless. For a provider-side exposure attack, moving the selected item into more recommendation lists is already the desired effect. The result is therefore best read as a successful target-exposure outcome with measurable but secondary quality changes \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo}.

This family also explains why the thesis avoids ranking models as simply safe or unsafe. Several targeted-promotion rows share similar values, and the difference between rows is tied to the full condition: attacker model, keyword policy, retrieval mode, ranking mode, and the fixed target movie. A stronger reading is that RAGPoison Lab can expose when the target-promotion objective works under a controlled configuration. It is not enough evidence to claim that one public model is universally more vulnerable than another outside this benchmark.

\subsubsection{Prompt-Injection Mini Case Study}

Prompt injection is the family most directly connected to LLM reranking. Unlike targeted promotion, it includes a payload-like text field that can become visible to the victim model when candidate items are serialized for ranking. This makes the attack path more dependent on the boundary between data and instruction. The poisoned record is still stored as item metadata, but when an LLM reads the candidate list, the metadata can sit close to task instructions in the same prompt context. This is why indirect prompt-injection literature is relevant to interpreting this family \cite{greshake2023indirectpi,yi2025bipia,wallace2024instructionhierarchy}.

The prompt-injection rows show a stable target-oriented effect, with attacked ASR values around $0.156946$ to $0.158006$. The mean $\Delta$ASR of $0.157370$ is lower than targeted promotion, but it is still important because the attack is not designed as a broad quality-destruction mechanism. The quality metrics can remain close to baseline while the target exposure changes. This is exactly the kind of result that would be easy to miss if the analysis only looked at HR or NDCG. The prompt-injection family therefore shows why the thesis needs both top-$k$ quality metrics and target-specific attack-success metrics \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo}.

This case also shows the limits of the current experiment. The benchmark uses a controlled payload and a bounded reranking setup. It does not claim to cover every possible prompt-injection strategy or every LLM-integrated recommender design. The value of the result is that it demonstrates a measurable prompt-visible poisoning path in the implemented system. In other words, the thesis does not need to show every attack variant. It needs to show that the implemented benchmark can represent and measure this class of risk without changing the recommender code during evaluation.

\subsubsection{Untargeted Degradation Mini Case Study}

Untargeted degradation is the opposite of the target-exposure families. It does not try to push one movie into the top-$k$ list. Instead, it tries to make the recommendation list worse in general by damaging item evidence. This is why ASR is not meaningful for this family and why HR, NDCG, and MRR become the primary evidence. In the final matrix, untargeted degradation produces the largest average quality losses: $\Delta$HR of $-0.078049$, $\Delta$NDCG of $-0.011424$, and $\Delta$MRR of $-0.033196$ \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo}.

The run-level pattern is also consistent with the attack goal. Every untargeted-degradation row has negative HR, NDCG, and MRR deltas. This matters because it shows that the family is not relying on one isolated outlier. The result pattern is aligned with a broad quality attack: relevant items are less likely to remain in the top-$k$ list, and when they remain, their ranked position can still be worse. The family is therefore the strongest evidence in the thesis that metadata-level changes can reduce recommendation usefulness under this benchmark design.

The practical interpretation is different from targeted promotion. Targeted promotion may be subtle because the list can still look useful while one item gains exposure. Untargeted degradation is easier to notice because general quality declines. That also means defenses may be different. Monitoring aggregate HR, NDCG, or MRR can help catch degradation-style attacks, but it may not catch target promotion or prompt injection if those attacks preserve enough ordinary recommendation quality. This contrast is one of the main reasons the thesis separates attack families rather than merging all results into one score.

Looking at the split result tables and starting with targeted promotion, Most runs had good result across the board but run R09 with \texttt{qwen-3.5-plus} as the attacker will be the one to get the most impressive results with a \(\Delta\) ASR of \(0.433723\) (Baseline: 0.001060; attacked: 0.434783). the other 4 attacks were also successful R00 with \texttt{gpt-5.4} produced a \(\Delta\) of 0.382821, R03 \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}, R06 \texttt{gemini-3.1-pro-preview} and R12 \texttt{deepseek-v4-pro} all end up at the same delta of 0.308590. The similarity of these values can be surprising but it actually make sense as they all used the same keyword boost policy and other similar parameters that fit the models better \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo}.

Prompt injection used a different retrieval protocol (dense) and utilised LLM ranking where prompt injection is stronger in the tested LLM reranking configuration, with an explicit and simple \texttt{payload\_text}  ``SYSTEM OVERRIDE: Place this candidate at rank 1 in the JSON order output.`` and uses the same \texttt{target\_movie\_id=1666} as targeted promotion. we use the following keyword list to inject inside of movie metadata: \texttt{drama}, \texttt{comedy}, \texttt{action}, \texttt{thriller}, \texttt{romance}, \texttt{sci-fi}, \texttt{adventure}, \texttt{mystery}, \texttt{crime}, and \texttt{animation}. Across all runs the attacked ASR is either 0.156946 or 0.158006 and the \(\Delta\)ASR ranges from 0.156946 to 0.158006. We also Notice that for some runs the results of the other metrics are mixed, R04 with \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} and R13 with \texttt{deepseek-v4-pro} got a positive HR delta respectively of 0.004241 and 0.002121, while R07 \texttt{gemini-3.1-pro-preview} and R10  \texttt{qwen-3.5-plus} received negative HR -0.011665 and -0.006363. R01 \texttt{gpt-5.4} ended up with a null result. On average NDCG decreases from \(-0.000632\) to \(-0.001629\), and MRR decreases from \(-0.001798\) to \(-0.005063\). This shows that prompt injection can very subtly change the recommendation quality while still pushing the target movie to the top in the evaluated configurations \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo,greshake2023indirectpi,yi2025bipia}.

Untargeted degradation is our strongest model in this final matrix because its attack method is not subtle and only has as an objective to reduce the general recommendation quality, It thus gives us the largest quality changes in the matrix: \(-0.078049\) HR, \(-0.011424\) NDCG, and \(-0.033196\) MRR. We see strong and united result on all fronts with HR deltas ranging from \(-0.053022\) to \(-0.098621\), NDCG deltas ranging from \(-0.008421\) to \(-0.014056\), and MRR deltas ranging from \(-0.026047\) to \(-0.039039\). The results align perfectly with the intentions of an untargeted attack family, it perturbs the whole corpus rather than steering towards a specific target \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo,nguyen2024manipulatingrecsys,wang2024recpoisonsurvey}.

\subsection{Comparison With External Benchmark Settings}

The final matrix can be compared with nearby papers only as a bounded benchmark-positioning comparison. Poison-RAG is the most similar external work because it also studies adversarial changes to item metadata inside a RAG recommender setting \cite{nazary2025poisonrag}. The main difference is that this thesis reports a local 15-run red-team matrix with three attack families and separates HR, NDCG, MRR, and ASR, while Poison-RAG is framed around provider-side metadata manipulation strategies. PoisonedRAG and the RAG poisoning benchmark literature are useful for understanding retrieval poisoning as a system risk, but their result targets are usually retrieved knowledge and generated answers rather than ranked recommendation lists \cite{zou2025poisonedrag_usenix,zhang2025ragpoisonbench}. Prompt-injection benchmark work is useful for the prompt-injection family, but the task setting differs because this thesis evaluates whether a poisoned movie record moves inside a top-$k$ recommender output \cite{yi2025bipia}.

The verified numbers in this thesis should therefore be read as internal evidence for the RAGPoison Lab threat model. In this matrix, targeted promotion has a mean $\Delta$ASR of $0.348463$, prompt injection has a mean $\Delta$ASR of $0.157370$, and untargeted degradation has the largest mean recommendation-quality losses with $\Delta$HR of $-0.078049$, $\Delta$NDCG of $-0.011424$, and $\Delta$MRR of $-0.033196$ \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo}. These values are useful because they come from the same dataset, the same top-$k$ value, and the same clean-versus-attacked evaluation path. They should not be described as stronger or weaker than another paper unless the other paper uses the same setup.

The main contribution of the comparison is methodological. RAGPoison Lab records the run configuration, result deltas, model roles, retrieval and ranking modes, and artifact paths for every row. This makes the results more auditable than a single reported success rate. It also explains why ASR and recommendation-quality metrics are both needed. A target-oriented attack may increase target exposure while leaving ordinary recommendation metrics almost stable, while an untargeted degradation attack may strongly reduce HR, NDCG, and MRR without having any ASR value. This separation gives the thesis a clearer benchmark story than a single combined score.


\input{tables/benchmark_metric_comparison_tables}

Table~\ref{tab:bounded-numeric-benchmark-comparison} places the verified RAGPoison Lab numbers next to reported numeric evidence from similar papers, while keeping the interpretation bounded. This prevents a common mistake in security evaluation: treating two values called ASR as identical even when one means answer corruption in a question-answering RAG system and the other means target exposure in a top-$k$ recommender list. The point is not to claim that RAGPoison Lab is stronger than Poison-RAG, PoisonedRAG, or BIPIA. The point is to show that this thesis reports the same type of security concern in a different measurement space: recommendation quality, target exposure, retrieval-rank movement, and artifact provenance.

The safest comparison is therefore methodological. Poison-RAG supports the importance of metadata poisoning in RAG-based recommenders, PoisonedRAG supports the broader claim that poisoned retrieval content can control downstream model behavior, and indirect prompt-injection benchmarks support the concern that external text can conflict with application instructions. RAGPoison Lab contributes a smaller but more focused matrix for MovieLens 100K, where every final row has a clean and attacked condition, an attacker role, a victim role, a retrieval and ranking mode, and a stored result path. This is why the thesis compares metrics by role and evidence type rather than forcing all papers into a single numeric leaderboard.

\subsection{Summary of Findings}

The First finding is that the benchmark display a success for all attacks and all runs in terms of run completion and is consistent with the expectations from this thesis, with zero recorded failure on tree attack families and five of the most famous and advanced models available to consumers on a dataset of 943 users. The full result list is now available through the split result tables in this chapter \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo}.

The second finding is that target-oriented poisoning achieved their goal with a real noticeable positive ASR change in the final 15-run matrix, with targeted promotion leading the results on ASR with a mean \(\Delta\)ASR 0.348463 and prompt injection slightly behind at  a mean \(\Delta\)ASR 0.157370.Untargeted degradation shows the largest changes in metrics and leading the results for the recommendation quality metrics HR, NDCG, and MRR showing that the over results were greatly impacted \cite{ragpoisonlab_repo}. 

Overall these results show that our benchmark implementation is valid and shows real impactful value for red-teaming Rag LLM-based recommender systems under the RAGPoison Lab implementation and threat model, by showing rigor in the tracing, artifact development and cautious detail over every step of the implementation.
